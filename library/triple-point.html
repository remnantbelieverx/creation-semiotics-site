The triple point

The old lesson says water is ice, then liquid, then steam, like Father, Son, and Spirit. That lesson teaches succession. The physics of water teaches something else, and it still does not prove the Trinity.

The public claim

At one pressure and one temperature, pure water can be ice, liquid, and vapor at the same time, in equilibrium. The Sunday-school sequence — ice, then liquid, then steam — puts the three forms in a line. The church’s confession does not. Father, Son, and Spirit are simultaneous Persons of one divine essence. The triple point is a better picture of simultaneity and of one substance. It is still a picture.

What the old lesson actually teaches

The sequence

One thing wearing three costumes

  • Ice, then water, then steam, in time.
  • One actor, three scenes.
  • That is the shape of modalism: God appearing in modes, not three Persons at once.
The point

Three phases, one substance, together

  • Ice, liquid, and vapor coexist.
  • They are distinguishable and not turns.
  • Closer to the confession. Still not the confession.

Modalism is not a fussy metaphor. The church named it because it loses the Son who prays to the Father and the Spirit who is sent. Correct the picture. Do not stop at the picture.

The mechanism, in four steps

  1. A phase diagram divides solid, liquid, and vapor by temperature and pressure.
  2. A boundary line is where two phases can coexist.
  3. Water’s three boundaries meet at one coordinate: 273.16 K and 611.657 pascals. A point, not a region.
  4. Only there are all three phases present together in stable equilibrium.

Until 2019 the kelvin itself was defined from this point. A sketch of the diagram is enough. No lab is required to say this, and no lab is allowed to say more than this.

Where the picture stops

We claim

A correction, and a real coordinate

  • The sequential water lesson is broken.
  • Coexistence at a point is ordinary physics.
  • The better picture is for teaching, not for proof.
We refuse

A diagram standing in for God

  • The phase diagram is not the Trinity.
  • The pascal value is not a secret code.
  • Questioning the old analogy is not denying the Trinity.

Fifteen to thirty minutes

  1. Say the sentence people already know: water is like the Trinity — ice, liquid, steam.
  2. Name what that sentence teaches: one God in sequence.
  3. Draw the triple point. Three phases, one address, at once.
  4. Read Matthew 28:19 or John 1:1–14. The diagram yields to the Persons.
  5. Say aloud: this helps, and it does not prove.

Overclaims to refuse

  • A change in the definition of the kelvin proves design at every standards meeting.
  • Anyone who retires the old analogy is unorthodox.
  • The triple point replaces Scripture.

Questions

  1. Why correct a lesson that “mostly works”?
  2. What is the difference between a picture and a proof?
  3. Where else do we teach succession when the text teaches presence together?
