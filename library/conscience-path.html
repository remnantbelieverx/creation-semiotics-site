Conscience, then the Verdict, then care

One path for a pastor or a careful friend: name the court already inside a person, walk the free examination, and leave them with plain next steps. No score. No countdown. The court itself is never behind a door.

What this sheet is for

Every person already carries a forensic structure — charge, defense, verdict — which Paul names in Romans 2:14–15. The free site does not invent that court. It walks someone through it. The gospel is presented as an exchange: the Judge steps down, bears the sentence, and offers his own robe. After trust, the path is plain: Scripture, a real church, one living confession.

This sheet unpacks that path for a mentor. It does not replace the pages. Send the person to the pages.

The words, at public altitude

“They show that the work of the law is written on their hearts, while their conscience also bears witness, and their conflicting thoughts accuse or even excuse them.”
Romans 2:15
katēgorountōn · accusing

The charge

“I did wrong” arrives without a policeman in the room. The thought prosecutes.

apologoumenōn · defending

The defense

Excuse, comparison, and the story that makes the charge smaller. The same inner court supplies the lawyer.

The verdict

The restlessness

A judgment has been rendered. People seek peace, or they live under the sentence. Cultures disagree about the cases. They still share this grammar.

Do not claim every conscience is equally trained, or true in what it condemns. The claim is the structure, not a universal syllabus of sins.

What we claim, and what we refuse

We claim

A real court, and a real exchange

  • The forensic structure is attested.
  • The gospel is a robe given, not a mood earned.
  • Care is three plain steps.
  • Church help is a human reply, unhurried.
We refuse

Pressure dressed as care

  • Any score of how saved someone is.
  • Countdown timers and hype.
  • Putting repentance behind a payment.
  • A fake list of churches.

The record is real, and self-payment fails. Christ bears the sentence and rises. Righteousness is given. The longer account of that exchange stays off this page. The examination already says it at gospel altitude.

If despair shows up

Stop the sequence. A person in despair needs a human being and a local emergency number before another theological screen. In the United States, call or text 988. In the Philippines, Hopeline is 2919. The care page says the same.

One sitting

About an hour. Invitation, not a script they must finish.

  1. Read Romans 2:14–15 slowly.
  2. Open the conscience page and name the three parts.
  3. Invite them to take The Verdict on their own screen.
  4. After the gospel screens, be quiet. Ask what was true.
  5. If they trusted Christ, open next steps: read, belong, speak.
  6. Pray without pressure. Leave church help open. Do not close the evening on a performance.

Across several days, the same order holds. Day one is the examination and, if they trusted, the care page. Then a chapter of John. Then a gathering, or the church-help form. Then one living person told. No one is late for failing to feel the right thing.

Questions for a mentor

  1. When did you last make an excuse that felt like a defense attorney?
  2. What is the difference between conviction and despair?
  3. Why must care include belonging, and not only a private feeling?
  4. What would “I trusted Christ this week” sound like in one sentence?

Overclaims to refuse

  • The examination replaces a church.
  • A perfect emotion is required for real faith.
  • Everyone who closes the tab is rejecting God.
  • Greek words are keys that work without the plain gospel.

Allowlist A-01 and the gospel exchange already on The Verdict. A teaching path, not a paste of the vault file. The front door stays free.
